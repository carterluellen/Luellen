    <p>
    H. pylori infection does not increase matrix metalloproteinase activity of human gastric organoids <br>
    </p>
    <p>
    Catherine Azkue Acosta 1, Carter Luellen 2, Jessica Rua 3, Andrew T Sebrell 4, Diane Bimzok 5
    </p>
    <p>
    Mentor: Michelle D. Cherne, Ph.D.
    </p>
    <p>
    Background: H. pylori is a Gram-negative bacterium that colonizes the gastric mucosa and infects
    approximately half of the world’s population. In chronic H. pylori infection, persistent epithelial
    inflammation induces expression of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), zinc-dependent enzymes involved
    25 in gastric extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling. The gastric ECM is a structural network composed of
    collagens, glycoproteins, and proteoglycans, and MMPs target these components for degradation. The
    implementation of human gastric organoids provides a physiological model of gastric epithelium which
    can be manipulated to study MMP enzymatic activity during H. pylori infection. This study investigated the
    effect of H. pylori infection on MMP activity in human gastric organoids and evaluated experimentally
    induced collagen damage using fluorescein-labeled collagen hybridizing peptide (F-CHP) detection.
    Methods: Four independent human gastric organoid samples were collected and treated on separate days
    per experimental condition: H. pylori microinjection, TNF-α treatment as a positive inflammatory control,
    and uninoculated microinjection as a "mock" mechanical injury control. Subsequently, the samples were
    concentrated fourfold using centrifugal filter units due to preliminary optimization that demonstrated
    increased fluorescence in fourfold concentrated human gastric organoid conditioned media. Each sample
    was plated in duplicates on a 96 Well Black Polystyrene Microplate and incubated with APMA to activate
    pro-MMPs. MMP activity was measured using the Amplite™ Universal MMP Activity Assay Kit.
    Fluorescence was measured kinetically over 60 minutes to assess MMP activity. Gastric tissue samples
    were separately treated with collagenase or heat denaturation, while untreated tissue and phosphate
    buffered saline (PBS) were incorporated as controls. Subsequent collagen damage was evaluated using the
    3Helix F-CHP assay.
    </p>
    <p>
    Results: MMP activity did not significantly differ between the H. pylori-infected and mock-injection
    conditions (27.90 ± 0.66 vs. 31.96 ± 6.33 million RFU; p = 0.3968). In the TNF-α condition, organoids
    exhibited greater MMP activity than the H. pylori-infected condition (37.75 ± 3.52 vs. 27.90 ± 0.66 million
    RFU; p = 0.0221). MMP activity did not significantly differ between the TNF-α condition and the mock-
    injection condition (p = 0.1808). F-CHP fluorescence was observed to be highest in collagenase-treated
    tissue (83.59 ± 4.54 million RFU), followed by heat-denatured tissue (51.13 ± 2.06 million RFU), and
    untreated control tissue (18.56 ± 0.69 million RFU). PBS, as expected, demonstrated minimal
    fluorescence.
    </p>
    <p>
    Discussion: Although it is well established that chronic H. pylori infection induces expression of MMPs,
    this study found that H. pylori infection did not increase MMP expression relative to mock-injected
    controls, whereas the TNF-α positive inflammatory control demonstrated greater MMP activity than H.
    pylori-infected samples. This finding may suggest distinct effects of bacterial and inflammatory signaling in
    MMP activity. Furthermore, a fluorescence assay using F-CHP was able to detect collagen damage in
    positive control gastric tissue samples, with the collagenase-treated sample displaying the highest observed
    fluorescence, demonstrating the utility of F-CHP in detecting the presence of degraded collagen in ECM.
    Future studies could determine whether testing additional inflammatory cytokines with H. pylori could
    distinguish bacterial vs. inflammatory effects on MMP activity. [9-19-26]
    </p>
    <p>
    Microarchitecture of a human gastric extracellular matrix scaffold determined by scanning electron microscopy
    </p>
    <p>
    Eli Mathew 1, Carter Luellen 2, Ava Richard 3*, Jessica Rua 3
    </p>
    <p>
    Mentor: Michelle D Cherne, Ph.D.
    </p>
    <p>
    Objective: Compare structural differences between Matrigel and human gastric extracellular matrix
    (hgECM) using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and determine whether enzymatic degradation can be
    detected and quantified by measuring Matrigel pore size and hgECM fiber diameter.
    Methods: Human gastric extracellular matrix was decellularized to produce scaffolds for a visualization of
    gastric architecture under SEM. To investigate the effects of ECM proteases on microarchitecture,
    collagen was treated with collagenase, and Matrigel was treated with trypsin, then imaged using SEM.
    Decellularized hgECM and Matrigel samples underwent experimental enzyme degradation. 2 mg/mL
    collagenase was added to hgECM for 60s and 90s, while Matrigel samples were trypsinized for 90s or 10
    min. Following enzymatic treatment, samples were fixed with formalin and prepared for SEM. To best
    visualize structures, samples underwent graded ethanol dehydration, were refixed, critical point dried, and
    sputter coated. Samples were imaged using a JEOL JSM-IT710HR field-emission scanning electron
    microscope. SEM images were evaluated for structural characteristics including fiber organization, surface
    morphology, and pore sizes. Matrigel pore size and hgECM fiber diameter were quantified from SEM
    using ImageJ with the Fiji extension. Measurements were compared across enzyme degradation duration
    and sample acquisition dates.
    </p>
    <p>
    Results: Imaging with scanning electron microscopy demonstrated distinct differences in microstructure
    between Matrigel and hgECM. Matrigel predominantly exhibits a porous structure, while hgECM was
    fibrous with a fragmented appearance. Analysis of Matrigel images yielded a mean pore size of 1.351 ±
    0.585 μm in negative controls, 1.051 ± 0.287 μm following 90 seconds of trypsinization, and 1.335 ± 0.462
    μm following 10 minutes of trypsinization. Although the 90-second trypsin group showed smaller pore
    measurements, there was no progressive reduction in pore size with increasing trypsin exposure.
    Considerable sample pore variability was detected, which may have been caused by sample preparation
    differences.
    Enzymatic degradation of hgECM with collagenase was observable under SEM. Mean fiber
    diameter sizes of negative control hgECM were 0.139 μm; following 60 seconds of collagenase, mean fiber
    20 diameter was 0.084 μm; the 90-second treatment fiber diameter was 0.108 μm. Collagenase-treated fiber
    diameters were smaller than the mean untreated fiber size. SEM visualization and quantitative assessment
    identified structural differences between Matrigel and hgECM and microstructural changes following
    enzymatic treatment.
    </p>
    <p>
    Conclusions: Human gastric extracellular matrix displayed a disorganized fibrous microstructure composed
    primarily of collagen, distinct from laminin-rich Matrigel. Enzymatic degradation produced measurable
    changes in both Matrigel pore size and hgECM fiber diameter, detected using SEM. These findings
    provide baseline evidence supporting the use of SEM in evaluating structural remodeling of ECM
    scaffolds. Future studies are needed to investigate whether similar patterns of enzymatic degradation can
    be identified in gastric ECM following inflammatory or pathogen-mediated responses, providing further
    insight into ECM remodeling in gastric diseases. [9-19-26]
    </p>
